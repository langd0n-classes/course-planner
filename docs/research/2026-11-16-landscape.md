# Landscape: planning and running one instructor's course

Research date: 2026-11-16. Scope: 27 products and tools across eight categories. Capitalized terms such as Course, Term, Topic, Activity, and Learning Module name this application's objects; see [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md).

## 1. Summary

- The strongest case for this application is helping an instructor recover from disrupted teaching while preserving the original plan.[^local]
- Meeting plans, outcome mapping, holiday calendars, and course reuse already have substantial overlap elsewhere.[^atlas][^planbook][^canvas]
- Atlas has intended and taught curriculum maps. Planbook can copy edited lessons into a reusable bank.[^atlas-admin][^planbook-actions]
- Coursetune links activities to objectives and records planned student time.[^coursetune]
- Canvas can pace assignments around blackout dates. That does not establish topic-aware recovery recommendations.[^canvas-pacing]
- Shovel combines syllabus ingestion, time estimates, weekly planning, and language-model access for students.[^shovel]
- Brightspace tracks grading time through a paid add-on. A future grading-load forecast remains unverified.[^d2l-release]
- Prioritize the daily teaching loop, selective recovery, and reviewed promotion of delivery changes into Course design.[^local]
- Use calendar and content exports for external delivery. Keep student records and institutional governance outside this application.[^local]
- No reviewed source establishes the entire proposed workflow in one competing product. That is an evidence gap, not proof of uniqueness.

## 2. Comparison table

**Ratings.** “yes” means the source documents the capability at the stated scope. “partial” means a documented subset or a manual substitute. “no” requires an explicit exclusion. “unverified” means insufficient evidence. ### Evidence and limits behind the ratings

**Learning management systems.** Canvas documents dated events, Pages for class-time detail, course-copy date adjustments, and calendar feeds. Its optional Course Pacing feature skips weekends and blackout dates. That is a real scheduling feature, although the documented rule sequences assignment due dates. It does not establish recommendations about which unfinished Topic to move.[^canvas][^canvas-copy][^canvas-pacing] Canvas also has course outcomes and rubric alignment. This supports part of coverage, but does not establish an I/P/A meeting map.[^canvas-outcomes]

Blackboard Ultra aligns institution-provided goals to content and assessments. Instructors cannot create those goals. Its calendar shows meetings and due dates, and its exported ZIP includes goal alignments and calendar events.[^blackboard-goals][^blackboard-calendar][^blackboard-export] Moodle lets teachers attach competencies to activities, move calendar events, and import course content without user data.[^moodle-competencies][^moodle-calendar][^moodle-copy]

Brightspace distinguishes content alignment from assessment of outcomes. It has a coverage workflow, but that does not establish introduction/practice/assessment sequencing. Its copy process supports templates and later offerings. Its October 2026 release documents course work-to-do lists and active grading-time tracking for administrators through Lumi Feedback. Neither due-date lists nor retrospective time logs establish a weekly student-effort forecast or a future grading-hours forecast.[^d2l-outcomes][^d2l-copy][^d2l-release]

Google Classroom shares due dates with students through Classroom and Google Calendar. Teachers add class events in Google Calendar. Exported classwork becomes editable copies in another class; changes do not affect the source. “Topics” here organize classwork. The reviewed documents do not establish the application's versioned Topic-action graph.[^google-calendar][^google-copy]

**Curriculum tools.** Coursedog documents outcome tracking, proposal approval, degree maps, and SIS integration. CourseLeaf CIM documents outcome mapping, edit history, reuse of historic content, and downstream system connections. Kuali documents proposal governance and dependency visibility. These are course/program administration capabilities. Course prerequisites are not evidence of dated Topic prerequisites within a course.[^coursedog][^courseleaf][^kuali]

In eLumen Insights, coordinators map courses and outcomes with optional attainment levels. Current and future maps support changes between assessment cycles. That is partial coverage and reuse, at program scale. Watermark documents Introduce/Reinforce/Master indicators, non-course learning activities, immutable map snapshots, PDF export, and view links. Neither source establishes class-meeting I/P/A sequences or planned-versus-delivered meeting revisions.[^elumen][^watermark]

Atlas comes closer to the teaching workflow. It has dated units, optional lesson plans with standards, intended and diary map types, academic holidays, and annual archives. Archived courses are read-only; users can copy units forward. Separate diary maps are evidence of taught curriculum, but the source does not establish a paired immutable revision for each meeting.[^atlas][^atlas-admin] Its lesson editor also offers AI generation and conversational revision. That documents content drafting, not a disruption recovery algorithm.[^atlas-ai]

Chalk/Planboard has dated lesson editing, standards tracking, off-days, lesson copies, and student sharing. Its documented shift action moves future lessons forward. Its paid ICS feed exports the timetable and links to lessons. That establishes calendar exchange, not round-trip calendar editing.[^chalk][^chalk-standards][^chalk-days][^chalk-copy][^chalk-shift][^chalk-ics]

Coursetune is a material comparator. It connects learning activities and assessments to granular objectives. Activity properties include student time, modality, grading level, and asset links. Activity reports aggregate time or points. A grading level is not an estimate of instructor marking hours. Weekly dated workload and immutable delivery records remain unverified.[^coursetune][^coursetune-report]

**Lesson planners.** Planbook reports standards by date and frequency, including uncovered standards. It offers bump, extend, swap, and date locking. Teachers can export edited lessons to a reusable lesson bank. This earns “yes” for basic reuse plus return to a master bank. It does not establish preservation of the application's three distinct Course/planned/delivered facts.[^planbook-coverage][^planbook-actions] Common Planner moves individual activities between lessons, shifts lesson sequences, copies planbooks, and publishes class homework pages. Its automatic standards tracking does not establish delivery evidence separate from the plan.[^common][^common-move]

**Syllabus tools.** Simple Syllabus documents LTI, API, and flat-file connections. One institution's help page links a guide to syncing a tentative course schedule into Brightspace. That is one deployment's evidence, not a product guarantee. Course calendar blackout handling, delivery revisions, and recovery recommendations remain unverified.[^simple][^simple-schedule] Concourse documents LMS syllabus embedding and SIS feed processing. Its copy article could not be read, so the reuse rating stays unverified.[^concourse][^concourse-copy]

**Course as code.** Quarto and Jupyter Book publish authored documents and notebooks as websites. An instructor can write meeting and topic pages and copy their source for another term. That is partial planning and reuse; these sources do not establish a course scheduling model. Jupyter Book 2 uses the MyST engine. Older Jupyter Book 1 `_toc.yml` examples need migration review.[^quarto][^jupyterbook] The small course-scheduler project explicitly models term dates, holidays, cancellations, activities, and assignment release/due dates. It supports manual moves and exports, but its README does not establish topic coverage or recommendations.[^scheduler]

**Student planning and workload.** Syllabuddy advertises extracting dates from a syllabus and importing the result into Google or Apple calendars. Its page does not name the exchange format or show recurring update behavior.[^syllabuddy] Shovel documents syllabus PDF ingestion, task time estimates, weekly planning, study-time logging, and an MCP connector. The assistant can change tasks and events. Its scope is the student's study plan; recovery of the instructor's teaching sequence remains unverified.[^shovel]

Rice explains estimates for reading and writing, with adjustable rates and explicit uncertainty. These help size work, but a course average does not identify a peak week. UBC adds scheduled versus independent activity categories and a downloadable summary. Its reviewed interface does not establish a dated week-by-week workload graph.[^rice][^ubc]

**Language-model planning.** MagicSchool generates a standards-aligned lesson from goals, available time, and teaching context. Education Copilot advertises unit/semester pacing, milestones, calendar context, and editable exports. teachology.ai documents outcome-aligned units with adjustable length and scope, plus source-document uploads. These establish drafting support. They do not establish a persistent, constraint-checked response to a canceled class. No reviewed source verifies topic-level recovery recommendations in these three products.[^magic][^copilot][^teachology]

## 3. What this application does that the others do not

The defensible claim is a **distinct combination**, with several parts still planned. Public documentation does not justify “no competitor does this.”

The application baseline is the Course/Term model in the accepted decisions. Older roadmap and architecture passages describe a legacy Module/Skill system. They also describe mock AI. Delivery status in this report is as of 2026-11-16; open GitHub issues hold the current status.[^local]

| Candidate distinction | Evidence in this application | Closest documented overlap | Evidence-based conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preserve reusable design, the adopted Term plan, and actual delivery as separate facts | ADR-0003 pins Course versions and creates immutable Term delivery revisions. This is the specified model. | Atlas intended/diary maps and read-only annual archives; Watermark immutable map snapshots.[^atlas-admin][^watermark] | The distinction is per-Activity revision identity and the daily comparison. Neither competitor source establishes that exact combination. |
| Recover selected Topic actions after cancellation or incomplete teaching | Design principles require simulation before apply and visible coverage gaps. A useful recommendation is still planned. | Planbook can lock dates and shift lessons; Common Planner can move activities; Canvas can skip blackout dates.[^planbook-actions][^common][^canvas-pacing] | Test recommendations that split, shorten, omit, or reassign selected content while showing coverage consequences. Do not claim that manual rescheduling is new. |
| Carry a delivery lesson back into reusable Course design without rewriting history | ADR-0003 specifies selective, explicit promotion into a new Course draft. The closeout workflow is still planned. | Planbook exports edited lessons to a bank. Atlas copies archived units forward.[^planbook-actions][^atlas-admin] | Returning edits to reusable material already exists. Previewing a revision-aware promotion with Term provenance is the narrower distinction to prove. |
| Combine Topic sequence, weekly student effort, and grading effort when judging a change | Topic I/P/A links are in the model. The Term page shows date-order findings. Both load views remain planned. | Coursetune has activity/objective links and student time. Shovel has effort-aware study planning. Brightspace tracks grading time.[^coursetune][^shovel][^d2l-release] | The opportunity is a joint impact view for the instructor. A combined forecast remains unverified elsewhere; this application has not built it yet. |
| Reopen a Topic when the instructor judges that teaching did not land | ADR-0004 specifies revocable Term completion with an evidence note. It distinguishes intent, delivery, and judgment. This is accepted design intent, not a verified shipped feature. | Moodle rates student competencies; Brightspace assesses student outcomes.[^moodle-competencies][^d2l-outcomes] | A course-level reteach signal without student records differs from learner mastery tracking. Its daily usefulness needs observation with instructors. |

Outcome coverage counts alone are not a distinction. Watermark and eLumen provide staged mapping. Coursetune provides activity-level objective links. Brightspace separates content coverage from assessment achievement.[^watermark][^elumen][^coursetune][^d2l-outcomes]

No reviewed source verifies an advisory warning that a particular Topic is assessed before introduction, or that a Topic prerequisite occurs after its dependent, using actual Term dates. Institutional course prerequisites and student release conditions are different mechanisms. Keep this proposed advantage provisional until a scripted comparison checks it.[^kuali][^moodle-competencies][^d2l-outcomes]

## 4. What the others do that this application does not plan

Ranked by likely value to one instructor. These are additions or refinements not in the project's roadmaps or open issues. “Not planned” does not mean rejected. Broad content authoring, notebook support, history, collaboration, student schedules, workload, and calendar feeds already appear in the plans or roadmaps; they are not new discoveries.[^local]

| Rank | Capability | Who does it and how | One-sentence proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read a syllabus into a source-checkable draft | Shovel imports PDF syllabi into dated tasks with time estimates; Syllabuddy extracts deadlines.[^shovel][^syllabuddy] | Add a preview-only syllabus import that shows the source passage beside each proposed Activity and date. |
| 2 | Explain and adjust task-time estimates | Rice exposes assumptions about reading density, difficulty, purpose, and writing; UBC separates scheduled and independent work.[^rice][^ubc] | Let instructors refine an effort size with an optional transparent estimator and an uncertainty range. |
| 3 | Control which parts of a plan students can see | Planbook's Student View selects classes, lesson sections, and visible time ranges; Chalk shares selected lessons or days.[^planbook-students][^chalk] | Give the planned student schedule a publication preview with explicit public fields and a visible date window. |
| 4 | Attach sources to generated planning material | teachology.ai accepts PDF, Word, PowerPoint, text, JSON, and CSV content and reports references used in generation.[^teachology] | Require future AI proposals to cite the course material or instructor constraint that supports each substantive change. |
| 5 | Read reusable standards by stable external identifiers | Planbook supplies standards frameworks; CASE defines shared framework identifiers and associations.[^planbook][^case] | Preserve optional external outcome identifiers during import before considering a standards-library service. |
| 6 | Calibrate estimates from actual grading time | Brightspace's Lumi Feedback add-on records active grading time by assignment, session, and submission for administrators.[^d2l-release] | Accept an instructor-entered aggregate grading duration to refine future estimates without retaining student rows. |

Rank 3 refines an already planned student surface. Rank 2 refines already planned effort sizing. Both have near-term value without expanding the product into an LMS. Rank 6 depends on instructor access to aggregates; do not assume an individual instructor can obtain the administrator report.

For a calendar export, use stable UIDs, explicit timezones, and update metadata. RFC 5545 defines these fields. Consumer tests must check cancellations, moved events, and daylight-saving transitions. An imported holiday is an event until an instructor confirms its scheduling meaning; no institution's calendar policy becomes a global rule.[^ical]

For a content package, export only the student-facing planned revision selected for publication. Do not infer that Common Cartridge can round-trip delivery revisions, Topic prerequisites, or grading estimates. Preserve those in the application's own versioned data. Test package support with the target LMS before committing to a full exporter.[^cc][^local]

## 6. Do not build

- **A gradebook, submission store, attendance ledger, or per-student mastery system.** Moodle and Brightspace already document learner competency and assessment workflows. Export Activity instructions and links to the chosen delivery system. Keep any imported evidence aggregate-only, as ADR-0004 requires.[^moodle-competencies][^d2l-outcomes][^local]
- **Institutional course approval, catalog management, or room allocation.** Coursedog, CourseLeaf, and Kuali serve the governance layer. Read approved course metadata or publish a summary. The instructor's term plan should not become a second institutional record.[^coursedog][^courseleaf][^kuali]
- **A general notebook execution and course-site publishing engine.** Quarto and Jupyter Book support authored computational material and web publication. Export schedule content and link resources into those sites. Keep the planned lightweight student schedule.[^quarto][^jupyterbook]
- **A multi-course personal study optimizer.** Shovel plans a student's study time across deadlines and available hours. Export reliable meetings, milestones, and suggested effort. The instructor cannot know the student's full calendar.[^shovel]
- **A campus-wide syllabus policy and approval service.** Simple Syllabus and Concourse integrate syllabus publication with institutional systems. Supply schedule HTML or a URL. Do not recreate centralized policy ownership.[^simple][^concourse]
- **A second general document editor solely for export formatting.** Planbook, MagicSchool, and Education Copilot can hand material to Word or Google Docs. Keep authoring that helps course planning; send final layout work to those editors.[^planbook-actions][^magic-export][^copilot]

Retain in-app coverage, recovery simulation, planned/delivered comparison, and closeout review. Those support the instructor's primary workspace. Export is appropriate for external delivery, not as a replacement for those planning views.[^local]

## 7. Confidence

**High confidence:** the cited primary documentation describes manual lesson shifting, standard/outcome alignment, course copying, several calendar feeds, published course sites, and student-time estimators. Atlas and Planbook are closer competitors than an LMS-only comparison would suggest.

**Moderate confidence:** the proposed combination may serve one instructor better than these documented alternatives. This is an inference from public sources. No product account, paid tier, or scripted workflow was tested. No performance, usability, or output-quality claim follows from a feature page.

**Low confidence / unverified:** a general claim of market uniqueness; cancellation recommendations beyond date shifting; paired immutable meeting revisions in competitors; selective promotion with revision provenance; weekly grading forecasts; dated Topic prerequisite checks; current feature access for an individual instructor in institution-managed products. Table gaps record those limits.

**Specific source limits:**

- Concourse's copy-content page could not be read. Reuse rests on secondary text only and remains unverified.
- Coursetune's API page could not be read. Secondary text describes REST/JSON and administrator-only access. Its activity help pages corroborate activity links and student time. API access and operation details remain unverified.
- Education Copilot and teachology.ai supplied public feature pages. Calendar constraints, export formats, and persistent schedule state are untested. “Built around your calendar” does not prove holiday-aware scheduling.
- Syllabuddy's small public page does not specify upload formats, the calendar payload, recurrence, or update behavior. Do not assume `.ics` from Google/Apple calendar compatibility alone.
- Coursedog, CourseLeaf, and Kuali documentation does not settle the instructor-level meeting workflow. Their broader product suites may have separately licensed tools. Do not transfer features from sibling products into these rows.
- Vendor help pages can contain old instructions. For example, Coursetune's activity properties article dates to 2023. Current documentation access confirms availability of the page, not that a new customer receives that exact UI.
- Moodle documentation redirected to versioned paths. Confirm behavior against the institution's installed release. Jupyter Book 1 and 2 also require separate migration checks.

**Human checks that would change the decision:**

1. Run the same recovery scenario in Atlas, Planbook, Common Planner, Canvas Course Pacing, Coursetune, and an AI planner. Cancel one meeting. Leave one Topic unfinished. Keep an assessment fixed. Ask for options and their effects on Topic order and weekly effort.
2. Inspect the history after recovery. Can the instructor recover the original plan, record actual Topic actions, and select changes for the reusable master? Check Planbook's bank workflow and Atlas's diary/archive workflow before asserting novelty.
3. Test one academic calendar with holidays, a substitute teaching day, and a timezone transition. Confirm what the tool changes and what remains an instructor choice.
4. Test a due-date change against student effort and instructor grading hours. Distinguish forecasts from retrospective grading timers and counts of overdue work.
5. Round-trip a small content-only package in each intended LMS. Check dates, resource links, outcome links, and duplicate behavior. Verify feed refresh and cancellation behavior in calendar clients.
6. Confirm licenses, roles, and instructor access for any proposed integration. Confirm each claim that rests on secondary text before treating it as an implementation contract.
